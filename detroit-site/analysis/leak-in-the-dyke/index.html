There's a Leak in the Dyke

October 09, 2026
On this page
  • 1. Accounts. One Desk.
  • 2. Both Parties. Same Person. Same Title.
  • 3. The Firm Across the Street
  • What Else Does This Firm Do?

On February 26, 2025, a compliance specialist named Renae Moore took the witness stand in Ingham County District Court. She was the first witness called in the preliminary examination of former Michigan House Speaker Lee Chatfield, who faces 13 felony charges including embezzlement and conspiracy.

Moore works for Dykema Gossett PLLC, the law firm headquartered at 201 Townsend Street in Lansing — across the street from the Michigan State Capitol.

Under oath, she testified to three things that should concern every person in this state.

1. 80+ Accounts. One Desk.

Moore testified that at Dykema, she helps manage more than 80+ accounts — political nonprofit funds tied to Michigan lawmakers and officials.

She was the assistant secretary/treasurer of the Peninsula Fund, Chatfield’s political nonprofit that raised $1.8 million from anonymous donors between 2018 and 2020 while he served as House Speaker.

When asked by Judge Molly Hennessey Greenwalt whether she had a duty to report concerns about problematic spending — including a strip club tab submitted as a business expense — Moore replied:

“No. Not that I am aware of.”

Chatfield’s defense attorney, Mary Chartier, said to Moore on the stand:

“This entire time, Dykema is getting paid to ensure the Peninsula Fund is compliant.”

Moore testified there were times the fund was not compliant with the law.

Sources: Bridge Michigan, Detroit News, Lansing State Journal, AG Affidavit

2. Both Parties. Same Person. Same Title.

During cross-examination, Chartier highlighted that Moore holds the same title — assistant treasurer/secretary — on another political nonprofit: the Promise to the People Fund.

That fund is affiliated with Attorney General Dana Nessel.

The same Attorney General whose office is prosecuting Chatfield.

One compliance specialist. The defendant’s fund and the prosecutor’s fund. Same desk. Same role. Same firm.

Source: Publicly available tax filings; Moore’s testimony and court documents, per Lansing State Journal.

3. The Firm Across the Street

Craig Mauger of the Detroit News wrote the sentence that explains everything:

“Dykema, which has a law office across the street from the state Capitol, handles the paperwork and tax filings of many of the politician-connected nonprofits.”

80+ accounts. Both parties. One building. One switchboard: 517-374-9100 .

What Else Does This Firm Do?

Dykema’s own website states:

“Dykema has been a leader in structural educational reform since authoring charter school legislation in the early 1990s, successfully defending the legislation’s legal challenges, and representing state universities in establishing and overseeing public school academies in the last 20 years.”

They wrote the law that created Michigan’s charter school system. Then they defended that law in court. Then they represented the state universities that authorize charter schools. Then their partners formed charter entities by the hundreds through the Michigan Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs.

And their political compliance team — the same team where Moore manages 80+ accounts — runs the PAC compliance for the dark money vehicles that influence who sits on those charter school boards.

The Vehicles

W. Alan Wilk is Dykema’s Political Compliance Team Leader. He has been with the firm since passing the Michigan Bar in 1995. His phone number is 517-374-9122. Moore’s is 517-374-9121. One digit apart. Same desk cluster.

Wilk has incorporated at least 7+ dark money vehicles through LARA:

Save Detroit Jobs spent $84,000 defeating a sitting council member and electing his replacement — who then joined the board of a charter school run by a superintendent with 9 criminal convictions.

Put Progress First spent $545,000 supporting Mike Duggan’s independent governor campaign. Then Michigan First Principles spent money attacking John James, Duggan’s Republican rival in the governor’s race. Both formed by Wilk. Same attorney. Same desk. Opposite missions.

The Dykema Federal PAC (FEC ID: C00342113) gave $4,000 to Barrett and $4,000 to John James in the same cycle that Wilk’s vehicle was attacking James.

The firm funds the candidate AND the attack on the candidate.

Sources: LARA entity search, TransparencyUSA, FEC, Bridge Detroit, Detroit News

The Co-Billing

In 2017, FOIA records showed that Dykema billed the State of Michigan $108,238 for the teacher 3% retirement case under Governor Snyder. Five attorneys worked on it. Two of them:

  • W. Alan Wilk — $433.50/hour (dark money architect)
  • Jason Hanselman — $374/hour (charter entity formation)

The attorney who forms dark money vehicles and the attorney who forms charter school entities work the same cases and bill the state together.

Source: MLive, FOIA records

The Revolving Door

Aaron Keel was the Assistant Director of Government Relations for the Michigan Association of School Boards (MASB), where he lobbied for charter school oversight and accountability. MASB’s official position calls for “accountability and transparency of the authorizers and management companies.”

Then Keel became Deputy Chief of Staff in the Michigan House Democratic Caucus. Then Director of Legislative Affairs at the Michigan Department of Treasury. Then, in 2023, he joined Dykema as a government policy advisor — now working at the firm whose clients the MASB oversight legislation would regulate.

He went from lobbying for charter school accountability to working at the firm that built the charter school infrastructure.

Source: Dykema, MASB Resolutions

Five Levels

Count the roles one firm plays in one system:

  1. Wrote the charter school law (1990s)
  2. Defended the law in court (constitutional challenges)
  3. Represents the authorizers (state universities)
  4. Forms the entities under the law (LARA filings, hundreds)
  5. Runs the PAC compliance that elects the board members (Wilk/Moore: SDJ → council → charter school board)

No other entity in Michigan occupies every level of the same regulatory system.

The Leak

The Dutch have a story about a boy who put his finger in a leaking dyke to save his village. The story is American fiction — invented by Phoebe Cary in 1865. In reality, when Dutch dykes leak, they don’t trickle. Whole sections give way. You don’t stop it with a finger. You stop it with a boat.

Moore’s testimony is the leak.

One compliance specialist. 80+ accounts. No duty to report. Both parties. The defendant’s fund and the prosecutor’s fund. Strip club tabs submitted as business expenses. Policy manuals written by the firm and ignored by the clients. And when asked under oath if she had any obligation to tell anyone:

“No. Not that I am aware of.”

The dyke is leaking.
