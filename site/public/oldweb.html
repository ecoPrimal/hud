🏛 OLD WEB ARCHIVES — the vaults

Wayback Machine

Nearly a trillion archived pages going back to 1996. Paste any URL and pick a date. The calendar view is the fastest way to watch a site change decade by decade.
1996–now archive

oldweb.today

Archived pages rendered inside emulated period browsers — Netscape, Mosaic, early IE. The layout bugs, the fonts and the load behaviour are all authentic.
1993–2005 emulator

The Old Net

A proxy that strips modern pages to what a vintage browser can render. Built for people running real 90s hardware. BBS simulator, VRML gallery, Netscape mode.
1990s proxy

OoCities (GeoCities Archive)

The mirror of GeoCities. Area51, SiliconValley, SunsetStrip — all the neighborhoods, exactly as they were before Yahoo deleted everything in 2009.
1994–2009 mirror

Restorativland

Browsable GeoCities mirror organized by neighborhood. Every under-construction gif. Every marquee tag. Every guestbook.
1994–2009 mirror

Cameron's World

A collage assembled entirely from GeoCities pages: every animated GIF, under-construction sign and marquee tag, arranged into one scrolling monument.
1990s art

Neocities

The living successor to GeoCities. Free static hosting, hand-made sites. The browse page is a live directory of the modern small web. The web that STILL builds by hand.
2013–now live

Wiby

A search engine that indexes only lightweight, personal, hand-written pages. The 'surprise me' button is the closest thing left to a 1998 web ring.
all search

Yestersearch

Search engine that exclusively browses Tripod, Angelfire, and other old-web hosts. Find the pages that Google forgot.
1990s–2000s search

One Terabyte of Kilobyte Age

Automated screenshots from the GeoCities archive. The best single feed for the visual language of the amateur web.
1994–2009 research

textfiles.com

Jason Scott's archive of text-file culture BEFORE the web: BBS ads, phreaking guides, ASCII art, zines. Served over plain HTTP, which is entirely the point.
1980s–1990s archive

Telnet BBS Guide

Hundreds of bulletin board systems STILL accepting connections today. A telnet client and five minutes gets you into one. The web before the web.
1980s–now live
