?? HADR  fixing the distortion

the problem

Every display surface has a shape. The content doesn't know the shape. It renders as if the screen is a flat rectangle viewed head-on. But:

  • Your TV is viewed at an angle from the couch
  • The Frame's streaming codec compresses differently across the frame
  • Edge pixels are more distorted than center pixels
  • Motion near edges smears more than center motion
  • Color accuracy shifts at viewing angles (IPS glow, VA contrast shift)

Valve already solves this for VR  SteamVR lens distortion correction IS a metric tensor transformation. The Frame needs the same math, adapted for flat screens at distance.

the HADR correction

Human-Augmented Digital Reality  adapt content to the display surface's geometry:

DISPLAY SURFACE TYPES:
?????????????????????

  Rectangle (monitor):    flat, close, head-on
  ? golden ratio columns: 14.6% : 61.8% : 23.6%
  ? no distortion correction needed

  Rectangle at distance (TV via Frame):
  ? viewing angle creates keystone trapezoid
  ? edges compressed relative to center  
  ? correction: inverse keystone + edge sharpening
  ? golden ratio adapts to viewing frustum

  Sphere (VR headset):
  ? Fibonacci spirals at golden angle 137.5°

  Curved visor (underwater):
  ? geodesic golden cuts along curvature

THE MATH:

  g_ij = metric tensor of the display surface
  
  For flat rectangle at distance d, viewing angle ?:
  
  g_11 = 1 / cos²(?_x)    (horizontal stretch at edges)
  g_22 = 1 / cos²(?_y)    (vertical stretch at edges)  
  g_12 = g_21 = 0          (no shear for axis-aligned screens)

  x' = x * cos(?_x(x))    (compress edges to undo perspective)

  where r = distance from center, R = max radius
