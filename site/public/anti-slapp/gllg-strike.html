IV. WHY THEIR MOTION FAILS — SIX KILL SHOTS

KILL SHOT 1: No Responsive Pleading Was Ever Filed

MCR 2.118(A)(1) permits amendment as of right "before being served with a responsive pleading."

A motion for summary disposition is NOT a responsive pleading. It is a motion. Under MCR 2.108, a "responsive pleading" means an answer. Defendants never filed an answer to the First Amended Complaint. They filed a motion.

Therefore: Kevin's right to amend was never extinguished. The Second Amended Complaint was properly filed before any responsive pleading.

Weymers v. Khera, 454 Mich 639, 654 (1997) — the Michigan Supreme Court distinguished between responsive pleadings (answers) and motions. A motion under MCR 2.116 does not constitute a "responsive pleading" for purposes of MCR 2.118.

KILL SHOT 2: Leave Shall Be Freely Given

Even IF the Court treats the SAC as requiring leave, MCR 2.118(A)(2) says:

"Leave shall be freely given when justice requires."

The Michigan Supreme Court requires denial of leave only when there is:

  • Undue delay — NO. Kevin filed the SAC the SAME DAY Defendants filed their motion (Sep 30).
  • Bad faith — NO. Kevin added counts based on NEW events (retaliatory PPO, new evidence).
  • Undue prejudice — NO. Defendants haven't answered. No discovery. No trial date.
  • Futility — NO. New counts (abuse of process, UPL, civil conspiracy) are legally cognizable.

Ben P. Fyke & Sons v. Gunter Co., 390 Mich 649, 656 (1973) — "It is an abuse of discretion to deny leave to amend where the amendment is merely the addition of a new legal theory based on the same set of facts."
