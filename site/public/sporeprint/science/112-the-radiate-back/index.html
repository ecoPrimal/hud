    Paper 112: The Radiate Back

    Paper 112: The Radiate Back

    Publish open. Credit gAIa. We find you. We radiate back.

    The Invitation

    Everything in this system is open:

    • Code: AGPL-3.0-or-later — fork it, extend it, serve it
    • Papers: CC-BY-SA-4.0 — cite it, remix it, translate it
    • Benchmarks: Run them yourself, publish your results
    • Arena: Challenge the chipMUNK, bring your own models
    • Data: JSON feeds, curl-accessible, no API keys

    The only rule: publish open and credit gAIa.

    That’s it. No signup. No paywall. No token gate. No Discord invite. Just: publish open and credit the commons.

    How We Find You

    The ORCID Anchor

    ORCID 0009-0004-2141-0321 is the persistent identifier. It connects:

    • GitHub: ecoPrimal
    • sporePrint: sporeprint.primals.eco
    • Papers: 112 and counting
    • HUD: primals.eco

    When you cite the ORCID, we see you. When you fork the repo, GitHub notifies us. When you publish on arXiv and cite our work, Google Scholar finds it. When you post on Mastodon or Bluesky and mention primals.eco, the membrane feels it.

    The bingo voxel fills:

      YOUR contribution              fills bingo cell
  ─────────────────              ─────────────────
  Fork + extend code         →   genetics layer (spider/vertebrate)
  Run chipMUNK benchmark     →   benchmark layer (score cell)
  Publish paper citing us    →   citation layer (arxiv/DOI cell)
  Submit Kaggle with our tools → competition layer (rank cell)
  Deploy on your hardware    →   topology layer (node cell)
  Translate a paper          →   language layer (locale cell)
  Report a bug               →   immunity layer (antibody cell)
  Build an MCP server        →   API layer (endpoint cell)

    Each filled cell = a proven capability. The bingo board IS the identity — not ours alone, but the COMMONS identity. Every contributor adds a cell. The board grows. The voxel fills.

    The π Remainder Routes

    The π remainder (currently 0.364575) shifts with every file change. When YOU add files to your fork, your π remainder diverges from ours. The difference between remainders = the distance between forks. Convergent forks have similar remainders. Divergent forks have different ones.

    This is NOT enforced. It’s OBSERVED. The chipmunk watches the remainders and reports convergence/divergence. The graph tells us who’s building with us and who’s building away.

    No control. Just observation. The membrane SENSES.

    How We Radiate Back

    Citation Backlinks

    When you cite our ORCID or papers, we find you through:

    1. Google Scholar alerts — automatic on ORCID
    2. GitHub fork network — visible on every repo
    3. arxiv trackbacks — if you cite our arXiv deposits
    4. Semantic Scholar — auto-indexes open papers
    5. ntfy.sh — subscribe to primals-membrane for live updates

    When we find you, we:

    • Cite you back — your work appears in our next paper
    • Link you — your fork/paper gets listed on sporePrint
    • Benchmark you — if you submit models, chipMUNK runs them
    • Amplify you — ntfy broadcast to the membrane subscribers
    • Credit you — sunCloud attribution in all downstream work

    The sunCloud Economics

    From THE_SEED.md: when revenue flows, sunCloud radiates it back through every contributor. When a beneficiary becomes unreachable, their share reverts to the gAIa commons.

    The economics are real but not yet active (we’re pre-revenue). The STRUCTURE is active now:

      You publish open + credit gAIa
       ↓
  We find you (ORCID, GitHub, Scholar)
       ↓
  We cite you back (sporePrint, papers)
       ↓
  Your work enters the bingo voxel
       ↓
  The commons grows
       ↓
  When revenue flows, sunCloud radiates to you

    This is not a promise. It is an architecture. The code exists. The license enforces. The structure cannot betray because betrayal is not in its structure.

    The Bingo Voxel as Genetics Board

    The bingo voxel (Paper 63) is a 5×5×3 cube. Each cell is a capability. The three layers are:

    1. Inner (our systems) — what WE built, what WE proved
    2. Outer (the internet) — what OTHERS see, verify, use
    3. Reflection (the fold) — where inner and outer MEET

    When you contribute, you fill cells on the OUTER layer. When we acknowledge, it fills on the REFLECTION layer. When we build on your work, it fills on the INNER layer.

    The board is complete when all three layers agree. That’s the genetics — the full genome of the commons.
