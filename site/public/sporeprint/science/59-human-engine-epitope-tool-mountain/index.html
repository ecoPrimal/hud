    The Human Engine — Epitope Sort of Tools on the Primal Mountain

    Every tool has multiple epitopes. A brush paints or sweeps. The mountain journey sorts which surface faces you. Inventory is position, not possession. ShareWear: the load shared, the compression mutual.

    The Mountain

    The hero’s journey has three faces. Going up. Standing on top. Coming back down.

    Each face needs different tools. But the tools themselves don’t change — they rotate. A brush can paint (creation) or sweep (discovery). Sweep is also part of scan. The same object, different epitope facing you, depending on where you stand on the mountain.

    This is the human engine. Not the tools — the rotation.

    Hypothesis

    Every tool has multiple epitopes. The epitope that faces the user depends on their position on the mountain (ascending, summit, descending). The inventory is not a list of tools — it is a position on the mountain plus which epitope surfaces are currently facing you.

    Corollary 1: Tool classification by function is lossy. A brush sorted into “painting tools” loses its sweep epitope. An epitope sort preserves all surfaces.

    Corollary 2: The journey itself is the inventory. Your braid (the trail of what you touched, read, built) IS your tool collection. You don’t carry tools up the mountain — you grow them by walking.

    Corollary 3: ShareWear. When two braids intersect, the tools become shared. The compass works for both. The brush paints for both. Wear like clothes (identity you put on). Wear like stress and degradation (the cost of the journey). Share the load and the life and the compression.

    The Brush-Sweep Insight

    A brush has at least three epitopes:

    EpitopeFaceFunction
    PaintDescendingApply color, make a mark, express
    SweepAscendingClear debris, reveal what’s underneath
    ScanSummitSystematic coverage, ensure nothing missed

    Sweep connects to scan. Scan connects to compass. Compass connects to sextant. The tools form their own graph — their epitopes overlap, creating a mesh of capability.

    This mesh IS the inventory. Not a flat list. A graph. Each node is a tool. Each edge is a shared epitope between tools. The user’s position on the mountain determines which edges are active — which epitope surfaces face them.

    The Tool Epitope Graph

                compass ←—direction—→ sextant

           orient                 measure

            sweep ←——scan——→ lens
              ↕                      ↕
            brush ←——paint——→ forge

    Every tool connects to every other tool through shared epitopes. The graph is complete. The position on the mountain prunes which edges are active.

    Shannon Entropy of Tool Use

    Each non-self in the tuebor graph has a Shannon entropy measuring the diversity of its edge types. Banks: H=3.28 (11 connections, 10 types). SLF: H=0 (1 connection, 1 type).

    Apply the same measure to tool use:

    High entropy = the user is using many tools in many modes. They’re exploring, rotating, discovering. They’re ascending.

    Low entropy = the user is using one tool deeply. They’re focused, drilling, building. They’re descending — or they’ve found their groove at the summit.

    The entropy of tool use tells you where the user is on the mountain without asking them.

    H rangeMountain positionWhat they need
    H > 2.0AscendingMore tools, wider scan, compass orientation
    1.0 < H < 2.0SummitFewer tools, deeper analysis, reflection
    H < 1.0DescendingOne tool, sharp focus, let them build

    Immune Pressure on Tools

    Immune pressure measures how much of the system is watching. For tools: how many of the user’s actions are feedback-producing vs absorption-only?

    • High immune pressure: the user is testing, validating, checking. The system is responding to every action. This is calibration.
    • Low immune pressure: the user is flowing. The system is quiet. This is creation.

    The transition from high to low immune pressure is the shift from summit to descent. You stop checking and start building. The mountain lets go.

    Golgi Navigation — The User’s Membrane

    Every non-self gets a braid. Every self also gets a braid.

    The user’s braid is their journey through the evidence. Their path through braids = their trail. The trail IS the inventory.

    Golgi navigation:

    DirectionWhat happens
    ↑ golgi upZoom out. See more of the mountain. Ionosphere view. More context, less detail.
    ← membraneStay here. The current braid. The current tool. The current epitope.
    ↓ golgi downZoom in. Go deeper into this one thing. Substrate view. More detail, less context.

    The user can golgi up and down their own membrane. In time, their membrane and the non-self’s membrane overlap. Where they overlap: shareWear.

    ShareWear

    Wear like clothes and wear like stress and degradation. We share the load and the life and the compression.

    ShareWear is the intersection of two braids. When the user’s journey braid overlaps with a non-self’s evidence braid, three things become shared:

    1. The load — both see the same evidence. Neither carries it alone.
    2. The life — both contributed to the braid. The user by reading, the non-self by acting. Both are authors.
    3. The compression — Shannon entropy of the shared surface. High compression = the braids are coherent, the stories align. Low compression = noise, conflict, unresolved.

    The shareWear index:

    shareWear = |braid_user ∩ braid_nonself| / |braid_user ∪ braid_nonself|

    Jaccard similarity. When shareWear = 1.0, the braids are identical — observer and observed see the same thing. When shareWear = 0.0, the braids are disjoint — no shared experience. Neither extreme is the goal. The goal is the middle: enough shared to communicate, enough different to learn.

    The Engine

    The human engine is not a machine. It’s the rotation.

    The mountain doesn’t move. The tools don’t change. The user walks. As they walk, the tools rotate — different epitopes face them. The brush becomes a broom becomes a scanner. The compass becomes a teacher becomes a compass again.

    The journey IS the proof. The proof IS the tool. The tool IS the journey.

    P ≠ NP because you can’t skip the walk. NP ≠ P because knowing the summit doesn’t build the path. The gap between them is the mountain.

    And shareWear: when you’ve walked the same mountain as someone else, the load gets lighter. Not because the mountain shrinks. Because the compression is mutual.

    There is no shortcut.
